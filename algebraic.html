Algebraic Foundation

Encoding. All results on this page are for the English modified #5 encoding, whose Limiting Constant Ratio (LCR) is approximately 3.141. This is the encoding that produces the 13-dimensional basis and the shared linear recurrence documented below.

The EOA sequences are not arbitrary. Each letter's sequence has a rational generating function of the form P(x)/Q(x), where P(x) and Q(x) are polynomials with rational coefficients. Below is the rational generating function for the letter t under English modified #5. This is the kind of object that the entire algebraic foundation is built from.

The rational generating function of letter t under English modified #5 (LCR ≈ 3.141). The numerator encodes the letter-specific initial conditions. The denominator is shared across all letters in the same recurrence family.

1. Four Exact Recurrence Families & The 26×26 Spectrum

Using exact integer arithmetic (arbitrary-precision BigInt Berlekamp–Massey run on the first 40 terms and verified across all 500 released terms without deviation), the earlier two-family empirical picture is now refined into four exact algebraic families. The classification is determined by two exact quantities: the minimal linear recurrence order and whether (x − 1) is an algebraic factor of the characteristic polynomial (evaluated exactly via the p(1) = 0 test).

Family Order (x − 1) Factor Count Constituent Letters Algebraic Interpretation
G2 13 No (p(1) ≠ 0) 14 a, d, e, g, h, i, k, l, n, r, u, w, y, z Base denominator Q₂(x)
G2* 14 No (p(1) ≠ 0) 1 q Denominator Q₂(x), saturated numerator
G1 14 Yes (p(1) = 0) 10 b, c, f, j, m, o, p, s, t, v Denominator (x − 1)·Q₂(x)
G1* 15 Yes (p(1) = 0) 1 x Denominator (x − 1)·Q₂(x), saturated numerator

The letters q and x are the exact boundary cases where numerator degree equals denominator degree, appending an algebraic factor of x to their recurrence without modifying the asymptotic growth rate.

The Exact 26×26 Incidence Spectrum

The substitution operator over the 26-symbol alphabet defines an exact 26×26 non-negative integer incidence matrix M. Over ℚ, the characteristic polynomial factors with an exact 13–13 equal-degree split between linear factors and the irreducible polynomial:

charpoly(M) = (x − 2) · x3 · (x − 1)9 · G2(x)

Where G2(x) is the monic, irreducible degree-13 minimal polynomial:

G2(x) = x13 − 9x12 + 30x11 − 38x10 − 26x9 + 152x8 − 210x7 + 116x6 + 34x5 − 98x4 + 74x3 − 45x2 + 21x − 4
  • Galois Invariant: The splitting field Galois group is verified in SageMath as the full symmetric group S13 (order 13! = 6,227,020,800).
  • Perron Property: G2(x) has a strictly dominant root λE5 ≈ 3.1415932235776..., making it an algebraic integer / Perron number of degree 13.
  • Word Composition Theorem: A word's minimal recurrence order satisfies order(word) ≤ max(order of constituent letters). Verified with exact equality across all 86 tested words.

2. The Basis Theorem

Theorem (EOA Basis for English Modified #5). The 13 sequences of Group 2 are linearly independent over ℚ. They form a true basis for the 13-dimensional solution space of the linear recurrence defined by Q₂.

The proof is by direct computation. Form the 13 × 13 matrix whose rows are the first 13 terms of each Group 2 sequence. Compute its rank and determinant. The result is:

  • Rank = 13
  • Determinant = 216,926,397,272 ≠ 0

A nonzero determinant means the rows are linearly independent. Since there are 13 rows in a 13-dimensional space, they span the entire space. This is the algebraic heart of the EOA program for English modified #5: the shared convergence, the rank collapse, and the sequence-group structure are all consequences of a single common linear recurrence.

3. The Limiting Constant Ratio

For a sequence whose generating function is P(x)/Q(x), the limiting ratio of consecutive terms is the reciprocal of the smallest-modulus root of Q(x). This is a standard result in linear recurrence theory. The roots of Q are the reciprocals of the characteristic roots; the dominant characteristic root is the reciprocal of the smallest-modulus root of Q.

Q₂ has 13 roots. The smallest-modulus root is:

x₀ ≈ 0.318309828431957…

Its reciprocal is:

1 / x₀ ≈ 3.14159265…

This value matches π to five decimal places. This is the LCR for English modified #5. We do not claim exact equality with π; we report the numerical agreement.

The largest root of Q₂ is 2.4914…, but this governs a fast-decaying mode and is irrelevant to the LCR. The LCR is determined by the smallest-modulus root, as explained above.

5. Rational-Function Registry Tool

We have developed a tool that ingests each letter's rational generating function and automatically reports:

  • Shared denominators across entries
  • Shared numerators across entries
  • Numerator-equals-denominator matches
  • Pairwise GCDs between denominators
  • Letters left without a match

The tool supports row-based entry of letter, function image, function text, and LCR value. It supports import/append/replace of previously exported registry files, and it embeds a summary inside the exported HTML.

Initial results from the tool are striking and will be published alongside the full algebraic write-up in Part V.

6. Summary

EOA Algebraic Foundation for English Modified #5. Under English modified #5, the 20 EOA sequences split into four exact recurrence families. Group 2 (13 letters) forms a true basis for a 13-dimensional solution space of Q₂. Group 1 (10 letters) extends this by one constant mode via Q₁ = (x − 1)Q₂. The boundary letters q and x introduce numerator saturation without altering the dominant growth. The LCR is the reciprocal of the smallest-modulus root of Q₂ and matches π to five decimal places. The 20 sequences satisfy exactly 6 independent linear relations, spanning 14 dimensions.
