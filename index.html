The Engineering of Alphabets (EOA) program investigates a class of deterministic symbolic representations derived from letter-name strings. The research spans symbolic dynamics, representation theory, deterministic systems, and the algebraic structure of phonetic encodings.

The recursive operator β. Schematic of the deterministic transformation that maps spoken letter names to numerical sequences.

What EOA Offers and Why It Is Unique

EOA is not a learned embedding. It is a structural primitive.

  • Training free and deterministic: No data, no loss function, no stochasticity, purely algebraic.
  • 16 element sequence-group structure: The 26 English letters collapse to 16 unique sequences, an unexplained algebraic reduction.
  • Rank 1 dimensionality collapse: Word vectors built from these sequence groups collapse to a near one dimensional line.
  • Encoding dependent convergence: The Limiting Constant Ratio (LCR) shifts with phonetic encoding, from 2.710 (Hebrew) to 4.128 (Greek).
  • Algebraic foundation: 13 sequences form a true basis for a 13-dimensional solution space of a common linear recurrence.
  • Fractal geometry hints: Recursive, tree like branching underlies the sequence-group collapse, suggesting self similarity.
  • 43 open questions: The program poses 43 open problems across 10 domains, requiring tools from mathematics, linguistics, and machine learning.

Core Observations

  • Convergence: Recursive operator β maps letter names to convergent numerical sequences.
  • Sequence-group collapse: 26 English letters collapse to 16 distinct sequence groups.
  • Rank-1 dimensionality: Word vectors built from these sequence groups collapse to near one-dimensional line.
  • Self-similar structure: Recursive, tree-like fractal geometry hinted at underlying the sequence-group collapse.

Algebraic Foundation

Under English modified #5 (LCR ≈ 3.141), the 20 EOA sequences split into two recurrence families. The larger family has 13 sequences that form a true basis for a 13-dimensional solution space of a common linear recurrence. The generating functions share denominator Q₂(x). The other family has denominator Q₁(x) = (x − 1)Q₂(x). The limiting constant ratio is the reciprocal of the smallest-modulus root of Q₂, and it matches π to five decimal places. The 20 sequences satisfy exactly 6 independent linear relations, spanning a 14-dimensional space.

A tool has been developed to ingest each letter's rational generating function and automatically report shared denominators, shared numerators, and numerator-equals-denominator matches across entries. It also computes pairwise GCDs between denominators and flags letters without a match. Initial results are striking and will be published alongside the full algebraic write-up in Part V.

EOA Program: Research Parts

Part Title What It Covers
Part 0 The Engineering of Alphabets as an Open Frontier Programmatic overview; poses 43 open questions across 10 domains; introduces the EOA program and its scope.
Part I EOA-43: A Rank-Collapsed Deterministic Word Representation Presents the 43-term sequence vectors, rank-1 collapse, and the EOA-43 word representation.
Part II Empirical Cross-Encoding Variation of the Limiting Ratio Documents LCR variation across 7 encodings and 4 languages; explores encoding-dependence.
Part III Transient Structure and Detrended Residuals Analyzes the letter-specific transient regime (terms 1–38) and what information survives.
Part IV Boundary Mapping on Downstream Tasks Explores applications in NLP, compression, and adversarial robustness.
Part V The Operator Beta and Its Recurrence Class Theoretical classification of the operator; formal characterization of the recurrence and the 13-dimensional basis.

Open Questions

  • What operator class produces this convergence: substitution system, linear recurrence, contraction mapping, or none?
  • Why does the 26-letter alphabet collapse to exactly 16 distinct sequence groups?
  • What is the precise, metric-backed definition of the rank-1 collapse?
  • Does the generation process exhibit a formal fractal geometry or self-similar branching structure?
  • Can we design encodings whose LCR approximates π, e, or the golden ratio?
  • Is there a universal ceiling on LCR, or can an encoding push it arbitrarily high?
