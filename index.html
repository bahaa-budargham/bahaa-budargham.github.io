The Engineering of Alphabets (EOA) program investigates human writing systems as deterministic sequence engines and discrete dynamical systems. In a global census evaluating 36 writing systems and 1,056 letters, letter-name recursions collapse down to 402 distinct integer sequences — 346 of which have never been catalogued anywhere in mathematics (including the OEIS), while the remainder yield classical mathematical families (powers of two, Fibonacci bisections, Mersenne, Pell) and exact algebraic invariants (φ², 1+√2, 2+√2).

Latest Discovery: 36-Script Sequence Census & Universal Invariants

A Global Census of Writing Systems as Discrete Sequence Engines (November 2026)

Evaluating the integer sequences generated across 36 human writing systems (totaling 1,056 individual letters) under the EOA recursive operator, audited term-by-term against the On-Line Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences (OEIS):

  • 402 Distinct Sequences: Computed to 500 terms in exact BigInt arithmetic. 56 match catalogued OEIS entries, while 346 distinct sequences are uncatalogued, each governed by an explicit integer linear recurrence.
  • Classical Mathematical Families Emerge: Generates exact term-for-term matches to the powers of two (A000079), Fibonacci bisections (A001906, A001519), odd-index Lucas numbers (A002878), Pell family (A048739), Mersenne numbers (A000225), and the silver-square recurrence (A007070).
  • The Small-Core Anomaly (0 of 250): Natural writing systems non-randomly isolate their growth into tiny recurrent graph hubs of 2 to 4 letters. In 250 matched random-alphabet simulations, core sizes ran from 21 to 26; none fell below 21.
  • Multi-Lineage Universality (p = 0.009): The golden ratio squared (φ² ≈ 2.618) recurs across three historically independent writing traditions (Semitic, Brahmic, and Japonic). Against an exhaustive universe of 4,482 non-negative 3×3 matrices, this cross-lineage recurrence is statistically significant at p = 0.009 (1 in 110).

What EOA Offers and Why It Is Unique

EOA is not a learned embedding. It is a structural primitive.

  • Global 36-Script Census (1,056 letters → 346 uncatalogued sequences): Auditing 1,056 letters across 36 writing systems revealed that naming rules collapse into 402 distinct sequences. 346 of these sequences appear in no mathematical catalogue, despite every single one satisfying an explicit, solvable integer linear recurrence.
  • Training free and deterministic: No data, no loss function, no stochasticity, purely algebraic.
  • 16 element sequence-group structure: The 26 English letters collapse to 16 unique sequences, an unexplained algebraic reduction.
  • Rank 1 dimensionality collapse: Word vectors built from these sequence groups collapse to a near one dimensional line.
  • Encoding dependent convergence: The Limiting Constant Ratio (LCR) shifts with phonetic encoding, from 2.710 (Hebrew) to 4.128 (Greek).
  • Algebraic foundation: 13 sequences form a true basis for a 13-dimensional solution space of a common linear recurrence.
  • Fractal geometry hints: Recursive, tree like branching underlies the sequence-group collapse, suggesting self similarity.
  • 43 open questions: The program poses 43 open problems across 10 domains, requiring tools from mathematics, linguistics, and machine learning.

Algebraic Foundation

Under English modified #5 (LCR ≈ 3.141), the 20 EOA sequences split into two recurrence families. The larger family has 13 sequences that form a true basis for a 13-dimensional solution space of a common linear recurrence. The generating functions share denominator Q₂(x). The other family has denominator Q₁(x) = (x − 1)Q₂(x). The limiting constant ratio is the reciprocal of the smallest-modulus root of Q₂, and it matches π to five decimal places. The 20 sequences satisfy exactly 6 independent linear relations, spanning a 14-dimensional space.

A tool has been developed to ingest each letter's rational generating function and automatically report shared denominators, shared numerators, and numerator-equals-denominator matches across entries. It also computes pairwise GCDs between denominators and flags letters without a match. Initial results are striking and will be published alongside the full algebraic write-up in Part V.
