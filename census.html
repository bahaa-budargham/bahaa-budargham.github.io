The Alphabet as a Sequence Engine: A Global Census

What happens when human writing systems are treated not merely as cultural orthographies, but as deterministic discrete dynamical systems?

In this global census, we evaluated the integer sequences generated across 36 world writing systems (totaling 1,056 individual letters) under the Engineering of Alphabets (EOA) recursive operator. Auditing every sequence to 500 terms in exact BigInt arithmetic against the On-Line Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences (OEIS) reveals that letter-name rules non-randomly isolate into exact algebraic invariants and classical sequences.

2. Statistical Controls and Null Models

Proof Against Random Coincidence

  • The Small-Core Anomaly (0 of 250): Real writing systems isolate their growth into tiny recurrent hubs (2 to 4 letters). Across 250 matched random-alphabet simulations (2–5 letters per name), every single random alphabet became entangled in a giant loop (core sizes 21 to 26). None fell below 21.
  • Multi-Lineage Universality (p = 0.009): The golden ratio squared (φ²) recurs across three historically independent writing traditions: Semitic (Hebrew, Phoenician, Ge'ez), Brahmic (Thai), and Japonic (Japanese-Latin). Against an exhaustive universe of 4,482 non-negative 3×3 candidate hub matrices, this cross-lineage recurrence is statistically significant at p = 0.009 (1 in 110).
  • The English E5 Spectral Split (0 of 100): The 26×26 English E5 matrix factors into 13 linear factors and one irreducible polynomial of degree 13. Zero of 100 matched random matrices produced an equal-degree split.

3. The Uncatalogued 346 Sequences

While 56 sequences correspond to known mathematical sequences, 346 distinct generated sequences appear in no existing mathematical catalogue.

Each of these 346 sequences satisfies an explicit integer linear recurrence derived from its script's incidence matrix. They concentrate primarily in the large-core scripts (English E5, Greek, Gothic, Runic, Ogham, Glagolitic). Every sequence, its initial conditions, its recurrence coefficients, and its script attestation are released as open data in uncatalogued_sequences.csv for independent community analysis.
